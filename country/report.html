What is Cap/GDP? (the Buffett indicator)

A country's total stock-market capitalisation ÷ that same country's GDP — not global GDP. GDP is roughly one year of the economy's output; market cap is what investors collectively pay for its listed slice. So Cap/GDP is essentially a price-to-sales ratio for the whole country: how big the market is relative to the real economy underneath it.

Rough reading:

  • ~50–75% — market modest/cheap vs. the economy
  • ~100% — market cap equals one year of GDP; historically "fair"
  • 150%+ — market large/expensive vs. the economy

Rising over time means the market is inflating faster than the economy beneath it — genuine growth in the listed share of GDP, or just liquidity and exuberance. Where it sits now (latest, vs. 2004):

Caveat: Cap/GDP only works when the listed companies belong to the local economy. Hong Kong's 1,118% is meaningless — its exchange lists giant mainland Chinese firms against tiny HK GDP. The same distortion hits financial hubs (Switzerland, Netherlands, Luxembourg). Use it for large, self-contained domestic markets; ignore it for hubs. Euro-area data is also stale (ends 2018).
